Scores follow the head turn: the lowest, 03 and 07, are turned 56° and 40° from camera, and 07 also looks 34° down, where the two encoders disagree. The disagreement is what stands out, not either number. An earlier render of 08 scored higher on both encoders but did not read as her; the Lightning pass shown here replaced it, and is the softest frame because it decodes at half the size. The unrendered eyes in 01 and 02 sit below the 128 px scoring floor, and only a close look caught them. The skin in 05 is slightly waxy.

ModelQwen-Image-Edit 2511 in ComfyUI with the Qwen 2.5-VL text encoder. Shot 08 is a four-step Lightning pass of the same model.
IdentityHeld by the model from the reference image, with a prompt per shot.
FinishDecoded through a 2x upscaling VAE, then a Nomos2 upscale pass resampled back to size.
Face swapsShots 01 and 02: a ReActor face pass from the same reference, inswapper_128, where an eye had gone unrendered at 108 and 123 px, under the swap’s own 128 px output.
Identity checkUnder each frame: cosine similarity to the reference, antelopev2 then SFace, and the detected head yaw. Faces under 128 px stay unscored rather than shown low.
CharacterSynthetic throughout. The reference image was itself generated.

A rough scale for the numbers: the reference against a re-crop of itself scores 0.96 / 0.97, and against its mirror 0.95 / 0.92. What the numbers cannot give is a pass mark. In July the two encoders were checked against my own accept and reject calls on this character, and five frames rejected by eye scored 0.72 to 0.75 on antelopev2, inside the 0.56 to 0.97 range of the accepted ones. So a score here earns a frame a second look. It never settles one.
